2D Art & Concept

Image models for concept exploration, key art, paintovers, UI and LiveOps art. 2D is the most mature AI discipline, but many client contracts still limit AI to exploration and keep it out of final assets.

Typical gain: 40–60% (render-stage time; 10–20% across a whole project (studio-reported))

Where AI fits best: Exploring many concept options, combining multiple references, unifying artist-made pieces, style-locked LiveOps and UA art volume.

Integration notes: Train a style model or LoRA per client or IP. Run open models (Qwen, FLUX, SDXL) locally through ComfyUI for work under NDA. Keep artist overpaint as the final step for quality and copyright.

3D Modeling

Image- and text-to-3D generators for blockouts, high-poly starting points and prop variations. Close to production for props and simple characters. Not ready for hero, modular or multi-material assets.

Typical gain: 10–15% (faster prop production overall (studio-reported); blockout steps gain more)

Where AI fits best: Blockouts from 2D concepts, high-poly hypotheses, prop and character variations, filling angles missing from concept art.

Integration notes: Generate in Hunyuan3D, Tripo or Rodin, then retopologize, UV and texture. Check scale, pivots, LODs and collision in-engine. Tripo and TRELLIS can run locally for work under NDA.

Retopo, UVs & Texturing

Tools that turn dense AI or sculpted meshes into engine-ready assets: retopology, UV unwrapping and PBR materials. Studios report their most reliable 3D savings at this step.

Typical gain: 30–40% (average saving on low-poly optimisation; up to 80% on one character (studio-reported))

Where AI fits best: Low-poly versions for engines, semantic UVs, tileable materials from a text prompt or photo, retexturing to a new style.

Integration notes: Use for repetitive, precise steps. Keep a technical artist checking edge flow before rigging, and check texel density and channel packing for each target platform.

Rigging & Skinning

Auto-rigging and skin-weight tools. Standard humanoids are largely automated. Garments, non-humanoids and hero deformation still need a rigger.

Typical gain: Minutes (vs 1–3.5 days to rig and skin a character manually (Tencent's baseline, vendor claim))

Where AI fits best: Humanoid NPCs, skins and outfit variants, characters for pre-vis, converting scanned or AI meshes into animatable characters.

Integration notes: Standardize on one skeleton (Epic, Mixamo or in-house) so retargeting works. Run deformation tests on extreme poses before approving any automated skinning.

Animation & Mocap

Motion synthesis, markerless capture, AI-assisted keyframing and facial animation. This tab has the largest studio-reported gains in the catalogue, and one studio grew its animation team rather than shrinking it.

Typical gain: ~88% (stance set from 26 min of mocap in ~1 week vs 2+ months; lip-sync for a new language 80% faster (studio-reported))

Where AI fits best: Stance and transition coverage, locomotion variants, lip-sync in many languages, previs mocap without a stage, player emotes.

Integration notes: Lock a retargeting skeleton first. Use AI for coverage and base layers, and keep animators on performance and polish. Treat markerless capture as previs unless it is cleaned.

VFX

Real-time VFX is the least mature AI area in the pipeline. Most practical speed-ups come from GPU simulation tools and AI-generated flipbooks. Cinematic VFX is further along.

Typical gain: No data (no studio has published measured VFX gains; every figure here is an estimate)

Where AI fits best: Flipbook and sprite-sheet look development, stylized effect concepts, cinematic character replacement, clean plates and camera tracking.

Integration notes: Use AI for look development and flipbook drafts, then rebuild in Niagara or VFX Graph for timing, alpha and performance. Budget the texture memory of each flipbook for mobile.

Engine Integration & Code

Coding agents and AI built into Unity and Unreal. Both engines adopted the Model Context Protocol (MCP) in 2026, so agents can now edit scenes, assets and Blueprints directly rather than only writing code.

Typical gain: 15–20% (faster delivery on UI logic, refactoring and integration; 50–70% less boilerplate (studio-reported))

Where AI fits best: UI logic, refactoring, scripting, API integration, getting up to speed on unfamiliar engines, scene setup, test boilerplate, commit and documentation work.

Integration notes: Use private or local LLMs for client code. Require code review on everything AI writes. Keep architecture-level code human-written, a common studio QA policy.

How to read the efficiency figures

Every number applies to one step of the work, not a whole project. The best studio-reported data (2025–26 case studies) shows step-level gains of 40–80% shrinking to 10–20% across a full project once review, rework and the steps without AI are counted.

Estimates

Where no figure has been published, the card shows an estimate scaled from measured results on a similar step, labelled Estimate. Use it as a starting hypothesis and log your own before-and-after time on a pilot.

Before you adopt a tool

  • Check the commercial licence and plan tier.
  • Confirm the client contract allows AI at that stage.
  • Keep client IP off cloud tools you haven't approved.
  • Log the provenance of anything players will see (Steam, China and EU disclosure rules).
